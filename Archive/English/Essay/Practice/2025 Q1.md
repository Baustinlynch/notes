The birds by Daphne Du Maurier is a apocalyptic novelette set in post WW2 south England. It follows Nat Hocken as he tries to protect his family and cope with a drastic change in the weather and the sudden violent behaviour of the birds around him. Throughout the story the theme of human conflict with nature is strong, Nat is being attacked by both the strong and violent weather, and by the birds. When Du Maurier wrote The Birds, it was designed as an allegory for nuclear war, but i am going to be looking at it through a modern lens, the theme of human conflict with nature. **We will explore this theme by looking at imagery, characterisation, plot and setting.**

The motif of the east wind is used throughout the story **to forshadow the attacks of the birds**.

"The east wind, **like a razor,** stripped the leaves from the trees, **crackling and dry**, **shivering and scattered by the wind's blast**"

The motif of the east wind represents the sudden and drastic change that the birds bring. The east is a biblical reference; it refers to the plague of locusts sent to Egypt to punish the pharaoh for his miss-treatment of Moses and the Jews. The east wind also **symbolises change, renewal, divine power and judgement and new beginnings**. **But in the story, this change is not good for humanity**. This links to the theme of human conflict with nature very well; **it suggests that nature is personified** as the east wind punishes humanity for our treatment of nature, as the east wind punished the Pharaoh for his mistreatment of Moses and the Jews.

**Throughout the text, Du Maurier depicts the senseless, indiscriminate**, **chaotic yet calculated attacks of the birds**.

"**The terrible, fluttering wings. He could feel the blood on his hands, his wrists, his neck. Each stab of a swooping beak tore his flesh**" 

**This quote show the way that the birds are unforgiving** and **have an overwhelming scale,** bigger than anything that humanity can defend from. At multiple points throughout the story, **Du Maurier refers to the birds in a very militaristic manner, which helps to show the way that they are regimented and working together towards a common goal.** 

"**but they were not yet defeated, for as again and again they returned to the assault, jabbing at his hands, his neck, the little stabbing beaks sharp as pointed forks.**"

**This quote also shows the way that Du Maurier depicts the birds as a militaristic force**, They are not giving up, so to as a military force would send in wave upon wave of reinforcements after they were defeated in battle. **The rhyming of jabbing and stabbing only reinforces the overwhelming nature of the attacks of the birds, as the rhyme sticks in out heads much like the attack sticks on Nat. Further more the words "assault" and "stabbing" create a semantic feild of violence, reinforcing the fear of the birds. These quotes link back well to the theme of human conflict with nature because the way that Du Maurier has describes the birds as a military force, as nature becomes a weapon and is thus in conflict with humanity**.

**Throughout the story, Nat is grappling with the fact that he cannot save all of those around him and that some people just will not believe** the threat that the birds pose.

"**People standing about, shouting and laughing and getting drunk. "Come and watch the birds!**""

**This quote shows the way that some people will easily underestimate** the threat that the birds can pose, and underestimate the power that nature has over them. This links well to the theme of human conflict with nature, as nature is something that is all around us, and we often forget about it and **how humanity has been abusing and using it for own greed for our entire time on this planet**. 

**Throughout the text the symbol of the wireless is used to represent their link to the outside world as they slowly realise that no one is going to come and save them**.

""**At half past eight Nat switched the wireless off. (...) There isn't going to be any news." Said Nat. "We've got to depend on ourselves.**""

**The symbol of the wireless recurs throughout the text and represents stability and their connection to the outside world**. **As long as the BBC keep giving anouncements, they know that everything is going to be fine and that the government will come and save them eventually. When the BBC stop broadcasting, it is the point that they realise that they will have to fend for themselves and that no one is coming to save them.** It symbolises the loss of stability and the point in which humanity has lost this battle with the birds, and that they know nothing will be the same. This links to the theme of human conflict with nature very well as it, again, presents the birds as a military force that humanity has lost to.

Throughout the novelette Du Maurier uses the symbol of the fag packet to represent hope, human comforts and normality. **Throughout the story she uses it as a literary device to represent the loss of them**. 

"**He reached for it, switched on the silent wireless. He threw the empty packet on the fire and watched it burn**." 

Throughout the novelette the fag packet is used to represent human comforts, as it is something that people rely on. When Nat burns the empty fag packet, **it symbolises him finally realising that nothing will ever be the same and that the birds are something he is responsible for and must resolve himself**. This links well to the theme of human conflict with nature well, as it portrays the attacks of the birds like a war, with Nat being a civilian realising that his country has been defeated, and that his government will not come to save him.

**In conclusion, throughout the birds, Du Maurier seems to have been intent on showing how easily the safety and security of humanity can be destroyed by the most unforeseen threats. And how even when presented with substantial evidence people** will still not respect the threats posed to them, as they view them as not serious or something that isn't their issue, and that the government will resolve it for them. This text links very well to the theme of human conflict with nature, as it was made as an allegory for nuclear war, and can very easily be seen how both nuclear war and nature in this text are both unforeseen threats that the population don't take seriously.
