The Birds is a 1952 apocalyptic novelette by Daphne du Maurier. It is set post-WW2 in the south of England. It follows Nat Hocken and his family as they tackle the sudden change in the weather and a drastic change in the behaviour of the birds around them. In the story, he must defend himself and his family from the relentless attacks of the birds and reconcile with the fact that no one else will believe him about the danger of the birds. The Birds was intended as an allegory for nuclear war when Du Maurier wrote it. A key **THEME** from the short story is human conflict with nature. We will explore this theme by looking at setting, characterisation, plot and imagery.

The motif of the East wind is used throughout the story to foreshadow the attacks of the birds. 

“The east wind, like a razor, stripped the trees, and the leaves, crackling and dry, shivered and scattered with the wind’s blast.” 

The motif of the east wind represents the sudden and violent change that the birds bring. The east wind is a biblical reference; it references the East Wind that brought the plagues of Egypt to Pharaoh and his people for their mistreatment of Moses and the Jews. The east wind also symbolises change, renewal, divine power and judgment and new beginnings. But in the story of The Birds, this change is not good for humanity. This links to the theme of human conflict with nature very well; it suggests to us that nature is personified as the east wind and punishes humanity for our treatment of the earth, like when God sent the plague of locusts to punish Egypt.

Throughout the story, Du Maurier depicts the senseless, indiscriminate, calculated yet chaotic violence from the birds against the humans. 

“The terrible, fluttering wings. He could feel the blood on his hands, his wrists, his neck. Each stab of a swooping beak tore his flesh.” 

This quote shows the way that the birds are unforgiving and have an overwhelming scale larger than anything that humanity can defend. At multiple points throughout the story, Du Maurier refers to the birds in a very militaristic manner, which helps to show the way that they are regimented and working together towards a common goal.

"but they were not yet defeated, for as again and again they returned to the assault, jabbing his hands, his neck, the little stabbing beaks sharp as pointed forks."

This quote also the shows the way that Du-Maurier depicts the birds as if they are a military force, as they are not giving up, sending in wave after wave of reinforcements, as too would an invading army send in reinforcements after they were defeated in battle. The rhyming of jabbing and stabbing only reinforces the overwhelming nature of the attack, as the rhyme sticks in our head much like the attack sticks on Nat. Furthermore the words "assault" and "stabbing" create a semantic field of violence, reinforcing the fear of the birds. These quotes link back well to the **theme** of human conflict with nature becuase the way that Du Maurier has described the birds as a military force, as nature becomes a weapon and is thus in conflict with humanity.

Throughout the story, Nat is grappling with the fact that he cannot save all of those around him and that some people just will not believe what is happening. 

“People standing about, shouting and laughing, getting drunk. “Come and watch the birds!”” 

This quote shows the way that people will very easily underestimate the consequences of something that is happening and deny even the most concrete of evidence. This links well to the **theme** of human conflict with nature, as nature is something that is all around us all the time and it is something that humanity has been abusing and using for our own greed for our entire time on this planet.

Throughout the story of the symbol of the Wireless is used to demonstrate their link to the rest of the country as they slowly realise that no one is coming to save them. 

“At half past eight Nat switched the wireless off. (…) There isn’t going to be any news.” Said Nat. “We’ve got to depend on ourselves.” “ 

The symbol of the wireless recurs throughout the story and represents stability and human technology. As long as the BBC keep giving out announcements, they know that everything is going to be fine and that the government will come and help them eventually. When the BBC stops broadcasting, it is the point at which they realise that they will have to fend for themselves and that no one is coming to help them. It symbolises the loss of stability and the turning point when they know that nothing will ever be the same. 

Throughout the novelette, Du Maurier uses the symbol of the cigarette packet to represent hope, human comforts and normality and through the story, she uses it as a literary device to represent the loss of them.

“He reached for it, switched on the silent wireless. He threw the empty packet on the fire and watched it burn.”

Throughout the novelette, the cigarette packet is used to represent human comforts, as it is something that people rely on for comfort. When Nat burns the cigarette packet, it symbolises him finally realising that nothing will ever be the same and that the birds are something that he is responsible for and that won’t fix itself on its own. This links very well with the climate change interpretation, with more and more people every day realising that they are going to have to do something about it and that we cannot stagnate and wait for someone else to fix these problems.

In conclusion, throughout The Birds, Du Mauier seems to have been been intent on showing how easily the safety and security of humanity can be destroyed by the most unforeseen threats. And how even when presented by substantial evidence people still will not believe and respect a threat if it even remotely doesn’t line up with the way they think things are supposed to go. This novelette works very well as an allegory for the climate crisis, outlining problems we still have with our society today.
