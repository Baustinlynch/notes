
## National 5 RUAE Cheat Sheet


---

### **1. General Approach**

- **Read the question carefully**—know exactly what is being asked.
- **Use bullet points** for clarity, especially when more than one mark is available.
- **Quote and explain**: Always reference the text, then comment in your own words.
- **Don’t just repeat the passage**—show understanding by explaining or putting things in your own words[^2][^6].

---

### **2. Question Types \& How to Answer**

#### **Word Choice**

- **Quote** a single word or short phrase.
- **Explain** what it suggests (connotations/associations).
- **Link** to the question focus (e.g., mood, character, setting).
    - *E.g.*: “anonymous” suggests the building is bland and unremarkable, making the place seem miserable.


#### **Own Words**

- **Find the key idea(s)** in the passage.
- **Put it in your own words**—don’t just copy.
- **One point per mark**.
    - *E.g.*: If the passage says “the weather was appalling,” you might write: “The weather was very bad.”


#### **Link Questions**

- **Quote the linking sentence/phrase**.
- **Explain how it links back** to what came before.
- **Explain how it links forward** to what comes next.
    - *E.g.*: “This sentence links back to the previous paragraph about pollution and introduces the next section about its effects on health.”


#### **Sentence Structure**

- **Identify a feature** (e.g., list, repetition, short sentence, punctuation).
- **Quote or describe it**.
- **Explain its effect** or why the writer used it.
    - *E.g.*: “The use of a list emphasises the large number of problems.”


#### **Imagery (Simile/Metaphor)**

- **Quote the image**.
- **Say what type it is** (simile, metaphor, personification).
- **Explain what is being compared to what**.
- **Explain the effect** (what does it help you understand?).
    - *E.g.*: “The writer compares the city to a jungle, suggesting it is wild and dangerous.”


#### **Tone**

- **Identify the tone** (e.g., humorous, angry, sarcastic).
- **Quote a word or phrase** that shows this.
- **Explain how the word/phrase creates this tone**.


#### **Summary/Main Points**

- **Identify the key points** in the passage.
- **Write them in your own words**.
- **One point per mark**.

---

### **3. Marking Tips**

- **Each mark = one clear point** (quote + explanation, or own worded point)[^2][^6].
- **Don’t lift whole sentences**—marks are lost for copying too much[^2].
- **Be concise and precise**—directly answer what’s asked.

---

### **4. Quick Structures**

| Question Type      | Structure/Formula                                             |
| :----------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Word Choice        | “_____” suggests ____. This shows ____.                       |
| Own Words          | The writer means ____.                                        |
| Link               | “_____” links back to ____, and forward to ____.              |
| Sentence Structure | The writer uses ____. This emphasises/highlights ____.        |
| Imagery            | “_____” is a ____. It compares ____ to ____, suggesting ____. |
| Tone               | The tone is ____. “_____” shows this because ____.            |
| Summary            | One main point is ____. Another is ____.                      |


---

### **5. Common Mistakes to Avoid**

- Quoting too much or not at all.
- Not explaining the effect or meaning.
- Repeating the question without adding detail.
- Using the same explanation for different questions.

---

**Remember:**

- Always answer in bullet points for each mark.
- Use your own words as much as possible.
- Make your explanation clear, direct, and linked to the question focus[^2][^6].

---
