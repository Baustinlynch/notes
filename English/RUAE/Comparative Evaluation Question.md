### WW1 still has a deep place in the minds of the public.
Passage 1 states that the first world war still has a "powerful grip on our collective imagination", and Passage 2 states that even after so long since the first world war, it is still deeply ingrained in our media and that it's presence has "never been greater".

### The effects of the war can still be directly seen even today.
Passage 1 says that, regarding the first world war, Britain continues to speak of a "lost generation", and passage 2 states that, in his small French town, with its own history of sacrifice during the great war, "It's almost impossible to imagine the effect on this tiny community of these fatalities over the years"

### That for European countries, war will never be the same
Passage 1 states that Britain, and the British public have been "cast out of our pre-war Eden", and passage 2 states that "no society today would accept the horrendous casualty count."

### That the public want to know what the war was *like*
Passage 1 states that "military historians can tell us what the war was about; although what really interests us is what it was like." And that this leads us to turn to media, created by those who experienced the war first or second hand. And in passage 2, he states that when he wrote his film about the great war, he was "obsessed with getting every detail right (...)" and that he wanted to provide to the viewer a "vicarious experience" so that the viewer could say "So this is what it was like, this is what they went through, how they lived, how they died."
