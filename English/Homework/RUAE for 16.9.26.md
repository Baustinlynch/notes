- "one I'd authorized like a mafia hit."
   Just as a mafia his is heartless and efficient
   So to does the narrator feel that they have called for the tree to be killed by a professional.
   The writer uses this simile to show how the narrator feels guilty, watching the tree be "killed" from her window, and she is paying for the tree surgeon to do it, so she feels even more guilty for what has been done to her tree
- "loomed over the garden like a storm cloud"
   Just as a storm cloud hangs over and darkens a large area of land, so to does the tree hang over the garden, and cast a large shadow over the garden.
   The writer uses this simile, and the personification of loomed, to make the tree feel like it is watching over the whole garden in an almost threatening way.
- "like God's arm on the ceiling of the Sistine Chapel in Rome"
  Just as God's arm reaching down to Adam on the roof of the Sistine Chapel, so to does the writer feel that the tree is reaching down towards them from the heavens.
  This shows how the writer feels that trees are God-Like beings, and feels reprehensibly about having it cut down, as they feel that trees are greater and more powerful than us mortals.
