- "one I'd authorized like a mafia hit."
  Just as a mafia **hit is** heartless and efficient, **so** the narrator **feels** that they have called for the tree to be killed by a professional.
  The **writer** uses this simile to show how the narrator feels guilty, watching the tree be "killed" from her window, and she is paying for the tree surgeon to do it, so she feels even more guilty for what has been done to her tree.
- "loomed over the garden like a storm cloud"
  Just as a storm cloud hangs over and darkens a large area of land, **so** the tree **hangs** over the garden, and casts a large shadow over the garden.
  The **writer** uses this simile, and the personification of "loomed", to make the tree feel like it is watching over the whole garden in an almost threatening way.
- "like God's arm on the ceiling of the Sistine Chapel in Rome"
  Just as God's arm reaches down to Adam on the roof of the Sistine Chapel, **so** the **writer** **feels** that the tree is reaching down towards them from the heavens.
  This shows how the **writer** feels that trees are God-like beings, and feels **remorseful** about having it cut down, as they feel that trees are greater and more powerful than us mortals.
