### Character
#### Key traits
- Stoic, dutiful, shaped by grief
- He feels guilty for leaving the monastery; he feels that's why he was the only one to survive
- Survivor of catastrophic loss
- Spiritually serious; morally conscientious
- Educated but interrupted by circumstance
- The mix of Latin and old-Scots language helps to show that he still holds on to his monastery education
- Driven by responsibility even when alone
- He still buries the bodies even when everyone is dead and he doesn't need to
#### Higher-Level Insight
- Mal represents **historical loneliness** — he and the narrator are both lonely — but their loneliness comes about from completely different ways
- (Compare with [[Andrina/Characters|Bill Torvald]], whose loneliness is eased by Andrina's visits.)
### Narrator
#### Key traits
- Emotionally fragile; anxious; self-protective
- Lives a life of routine and avoidance
- Detached from workplace and social world
- Uses humour and rationalisation to cope
- Longs for connection but fears vulnerability
#### Higher-Level Insight
- The speaker represents **modern disconnection** — the modern loneliness epidemic — a loneliness created not by catastrophe but by the quiet erosion of meaningful relationships. The speaker and Mal are both on practically the same level of loneliness, but they are lonely for completely opposite reasons
