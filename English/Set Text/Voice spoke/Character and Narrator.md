### Character
#### Key traits
- Stoic, dutiful, shaped by grief
	- he feels guilty for leaving the monestary, he feels thats why he was the only one to survive
- Survivor of catastrophic loss
- Spiritually serious; morally conscientious
- Educated but interupted by circumstance
	- the mix of latin and old-scots language helps to show that he still holds on to his monestary education
- Driven by responsibility even when alone
	- he still burys the bodys even when everyone is dead and he doesnt need to
#### Higher-Level Insight
- Mal represents **historical loneliness** — he and the narrator are both lonely — but their lonelyness comes about from compleatly different ways
- (Compare with [[Andrina/Characters|Bill Torvald]], whose loneliness is eased by Andrina's visits.)
### Narator 
#### Key traits
- Emotionally fragile; anxious; self-protective
- Lives a life of routine and avoidance
- Detached from workplace and social world
- Uses humour and rationalisation to cope
- Longs for connection but fears vulnerability
#### Higher-Level insight
- The speaker represents **modern disconection** - the modern lonelyness epidemic - a lonelyness created not by catastrophy but by the quiet erosion of meaningfull relationships. The speaker and Mal-something are both on practicaly the same level of lonelyness, but they are lonely for compleatly oposite reasons
