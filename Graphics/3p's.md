# Preliminary graphics
Used in the planning stages of a product. Often consists of 'concept' sketches which are **quick,small** and **rough** sketches showing your ideas. Gantt charts can also be used in this stage to plan the tasks involved in the project
### Advantages
- Quick to produce
- Can be a good way of recording designs
- Can be produced cheaply
- Useful when communication ideas to a client or colleague

### Disadvantages
- Not drawn to scale
- Not dimensionally accurate

# Production graphics
Used by architects, engineers and manufacturers to make a product and therefore **must** contain as much info as possible about the product. This can include dimensions, moving parts, and material choices and can be shown on orthographic views, sectional views, exploded drawings, assemblies etc.
### Advantages
- They are important when the parts are to be manufactured
- Can show how components are assembled
- Drawing standards are used worldwide
- Understood in all languages
- A CAD library can be used
### Disadvantages
- Training or knowledge is required to produce them as drawing standards must be learnt
- They are time-consuming to produce
- Costly specialist equipment is required

# Promotional graphics
Used by sales and marketing professionals to **advertise** a product and can be in the form of brochures, posters, leaflets, displays etc. The must be eye catching and effective.

### Advantages
- They can be animated to create visual appeal
- Can be placed in a virtual enviroment to enhance realism and to give a greater sense of believability
- They can show the customer what the product will look like, for example, material, highlights, shadows etc.
- They can be used to advertise products
### Disadvantages
- You need skill to produce them
- They can give a false impression of the product
- Cant physically touch the rendered model
