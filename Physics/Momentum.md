The momentum of an object depends on the mass and velocity of an object.

![[P=mv.excalidraw]]

Momentum is measured in $kgms^{-1}$, kilogram meters per second.
Momentum is a **vector**, so direction matters!, if something is travelling away from you, you don't need to worry about it hitting you.
In collisions or explosions there is the principle of the conservation of momentum. You can be asked to state it; "The total momentum before a collision is equal to the total momentum after, in the absence of external forces"
(This principle is applied further in [[Elastic and Inelastic Collisions]].)

## Examples
1. 2 vehicles, one stationary, the other collides with it and they stick together in the collision. Find the velocity after the collision.

2. One ball "$a$" collides with a larger stationary ball"$b$".
   $b$ moves forward, $a$ rebounds.
   Find the final velocity of "$a$"
   ![[Momentum diagram.excalidraw]]
   
$$m_{a}u_{a}+m_{b}u_{b}=m_{a}v_{a}+m_{b}v_{b}$$
$$6\times 0.2+0.3\times 0=0.2\times v_{a}+0.5\times 5$$
$$1.2=0.2\times v_{a}+2.5$$
$$1.3=0.2\times v_{a}$$
$$v_{a}=\frac{1.3}{0.2}= 6.5ms^{-1}$$
