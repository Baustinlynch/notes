If a source of sound is moving **towards** you, the frequency you observe will be **higher** than if it was stationary.
If it is moving **away** from you, the frequency will appear **lower**.
$$f_{o}=f_{s}\left( \frac{v}{v\pm v_{s}} \right)$$
$$\text{frequency observed} = \text{frequency of the source} \times \left(\frac{\text{speed of sound}}{\text{speed of sound}\pm\text{speed of the source}}\right)$$

## More Doppler and light
The Doppler effect can be used for RADAR, e.g. using speed cameras.
$$\lambda_{o}=\lambda_{s}\left( 1\pm \frac{v_{s}}{c} \right)$$
- If the source is moving towards you, the frequency **gets** higher so the wavelength gets **shorter**.
- If the source is moving away, **f** decreases so wavelength **increases**.
- Moving away gives "Redshift"
- Moving towards gives "Blueshift"
- The amount of Redshift is given by a ratio

$$Z=\frac{\Delta \lambda}{\lambda_{\text{source at rest}}}\left[ \frac{{\lambda_{observed}-\lambda_{\text{source}}}}{\lambda_{source}} \right]$$
$$Z=\frac{\Delta \lambda}{\lambda_{\text{rest}}}=\frac{v}{c}$$
