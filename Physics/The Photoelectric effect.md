The leaf falls if it is illuminated with UV light. This causes the metal plate to eject electrons (negative charges). The greater the irradiance of the light, the more electrons would be emitted.
White light will **not** cause this effect, no matter how intense it is. This would work for other metals; zinc is used as it is convenient.

## Wave/Particle theory
Einstein explained this by suggesting that EM radiation sometimes behaves more like a particle than a wave. These particles of light are called **photons**. Each photon has a frequency and a wavelength and an energy given by:
$$E=hf$$
$$\text{Energy (J)}=\text{Planck's constant}\times\text{frequency (Hz)}$$
From the equation we can see that $E\propto f$. As UV is of a higher frequency than white light, it has enough energy to pass to the electrons to allow them to be ejected. It is also found that the electrons either absorb all of the energy or none of it. If there is enough energy to eject the electrons, then the more photons that hit the metal, the greater the irradiance.
$$I=nhf$$
$$\text{Irradiance}=\text{no. of photons}\times\text{Planck's constant}\times\text{frequency (Hz)}$$

## Threshold Frequency + Work Function
### **Threshold Frequency**
$f_0=$ the **minimum** frequency of the incident photons which will allow photoelectrons to be emitted.

### **Work Function**
The work function is the minimum energy required to release electrons from the metal.
$$W_0=hf_0$$
$$\text{work function (J)}=hf_0$$
If the incident photons have extra energy, then this is converted to $E_k$ in the ejected electrons.
$$hf=hf_0+E_k$$
$$\text{energy of incident photons}=hf_0+E_k$$

## Example
1. Light of $\lambda=2\times 10^{-7}\ \text{m}$ hits an aluminium surface. It is found that $6.7\times 10^{-19}\ \text{J}$ is required to eject electrons.
   A)Find the energy of the incident photons
   $f = \frac{c}{\lambda} = \frac{3\times10^8}{2\times10^{-7}} = 1.5\times10^{15}\ \text{Hz}$
   $E = hf = 6.63\times10^{-34} \times 1.5\times10^{15} = 9.95\times10^{-19}\ \text{J}$
   B) Find the maximum kinetic energy of the emitted electrons.
   $E_{\text{incident}}=\text{work function}+E_{k}$
   $9.95\times 10^{-19}=6.72\times 10^{-19}+E_{k}$
   $E_{k}=3.225\times{10}^{-19}$
   $\to \text{can then work out their velocity}$
   $E_{k}=\frac{1}{2}mv^2$
   $3.225\times{10}^{-19}= \frac{1}{2}\times{9}.11\times 10^{-31}\times v^2$
