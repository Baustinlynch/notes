Suppose that an object of mass $m$, is acted upon by force $F$, for a time $t$. This will cause a change in velocity from $u$ to $v$.
Using $f=ma$ and $a=\frac{v-u}{t}$, we can combine them to get $F=m(\frac{v-u}t)$.
This can also be written as $F=\frac{mv-mu}t$,$F=\frac{\text{final momentum}-\text{initial momentum}}t$,$Ft=mv-mu$ or $F=\frac{\Delta mv}{t}$.
The 'change in momentum' is known as the **impulse**.
$$\text{impulse}=\text{change in momentum}=Ft=\Delta mv$$
(This builds on the idea of [[Momentum|momentum]].)
The units for impulse are $kgms^{-1}$(kilogram metres per second) or $Ns$ (Newton seconds)
When a collision occurs, the impact force is not constant throughout; the graph of the impact force resembles a bell curve. The impulse is equal to the area under the $Ft$ graph.
#### Example:
A pool cue **hits** a ball of mass $0.2kg$ which is at rest. It moves off at $3ms^{-1}$. The contact time is 0.015s. Find the average force.
$$F=\frac{mv-mu}t$$
$$F=\frac{0.2\times 3-0.2\times 0}{0.015}$$
### Impulse and Airbags
Airbags in cars are used to make the change in momentum (impulse) take a longer time during a crash. 
This means that the force of the impact would be reduced.
