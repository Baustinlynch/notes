### Example:
A train engine is pulling 2 carriages as shown below:
![[Connected masses diagram.excalidraw|800]]
The engine produces a force of $80,000N$
The friction on the locomotive is $4,000N$
there is a frictional force of $2,000N$ on each carriage.

### Examples:

1. Find the acceleration of the train
%% col-start %%

%% col-break:b:secondary %%

$F=ma$
$72,000=53,000\times a$
$a=\frac{72,000}{53,000}$
$a=1.36ms^{-2}$

%% col-break:b:secondary %%

$F^{}_{un}=80,000-(4,000+2,000+2,000)$ 
$F^{}_{un}=72,000N$

$m=35,000+9,000+9,000$
$m=53,000kg$

%% col-end %%

---

2. Find the size of the force in the link between the locomotive and the first carriage.
%% col-start %%

%% col-break:b:secondary %%

$F=ma$
$F=18,000\times1.36$
$F=24480N$
$F^{}_{total}=24480+4000$
$F^{}_{total}=28480N$

%% col-break:b:secondary %%

$m=9,000+9,000$
$m=18,000kg$

%% col-end %%

The spring is allowed to settle. The thread is then cut. What is the acceleration of the 6kg block.

When balanced the Tesion on the spring is=$T=W=mg$
$W=8\times9.8$
$W=78.4N$
When the thread is cut, $T$ in sping is still $78.4N$ **but** their is a new $W=mg$, $W=6\times9.8=58.8N$. After the thread is cut the $F^{}_{un}=78.4-58.8=19.6N$ 
$F^{}_{un}=ma$
$19.6=6a$
$a=\frac{19.6}{6}$
$a=3.27ms^{-2}$
