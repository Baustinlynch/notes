### Example:
A train engine is pulling 2 carriages as shown below:
![[Connected masses diagram.excalidraw|800]]
The engine produces a force of $80,000\ \text{N}$
The friction on the locomotive is $4,000\ \text{N}$
There is a frictional force of $2,000\ \text{N}$ on each carriage.

### Examples:

1. Find the acceleration of the train
%% col-start %%

%% col-break:b:secondary %%

$F=ma$
$72,000=53,000\times a$
$a=\frac{72,000}{53,000}$
$\underline{a=1.36\ \text{ms}^{-2}}$

%% col-break:b:secondary %%

$F_{\text{un}}=80,000-(4,000+2,000+2,000)$ 
$F_{\text{un}}=72,000\ \text{N}$

$m=35,000+9,000+9,000$
$m=53,000\ \text{kg}$

%% col-end %%

---

2. Find the size of the force in the link between the locomotive and the first carriage.
%% col-start %%

%% col-break:b:secondary %%

$F=ma$
$F=18,000\times1.36$
$F=24,480\ \text{N}$
$F_{\text{total}}=24,480+4,000$
$\underline{F_{\text{total}}=28,480\ \text{N}}$

%% col-break:b:secondary %%

$m=9,000+9,000$
$m=18,000\ \text{kg}$

%% col-end %%

---

%% col-start %%

%% col-break:b:secondary %%

![[Connected masses, string and spring.excalidraw]]

%% col-break:b:secondary %%

The spring is allowed to settle. The thread is then cut. What is the acceleration of the 6 kg block?

%% col-end %%
When balanced, the tension on the spring is $T=W=mg$.
$W=8\times9.8$
$W=78.4\ \text{N}$
When the thread is cut, $T$ in spring is still $78.4\ \text{N}$ **but** there is a new $W=mg$, $W=6\times9.8=58.8\ \text{N}$. After the thread is cut, $F_{\text{un}}=78.4-58.8=19.6\ \text{N}$ 
$F_{\text{un}}=ma$
$19.6=6a$
$a=\frac{19.6}{6}$
$\underline{a=3.27\ \text{ms}^{-2}}$
