In this model the **electrons** orbit the **nucleus** of an atom at fixed distances from it. Electrons at each distance have a **fixed energy value** - so each distance is known as an **energy level**.

**Electrons** can move from one **energy level** to another, but cannot stop <u><b>between</b></u> the **energy levels**. This means the energy of the electrons is **quantised**.

As an **electron** gets closer to the **nucleus**, the **electron** loses energy - so the **energy levels** closer to the nucleus have <u><b>more negative</b></u> energy values.

In this representation of an atom, the energy levels are shown by <u>horizontal lines</u>.
This is because it is the easier of the two representations to draw, it is the one most commonly used in test and exam questions.
A hydrogen atom has only one electron, but this electron can occupy any one of these energy levels. The electron may also move to any of the possible energy levels.
The **energy level** closest to the **nucleus** (the level with the lowest energy) is called **the ground level ($E_0$)** - An **electron** in this **energy level** is said to be in an **excited state**.
