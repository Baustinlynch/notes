If an object is sliding down a slope, we do not get the full effect of the weight. To find the force down the slope, we can [[Resolving vectors|resolve the forces]].
![[Inclined forces diagram.excalidraw]]
Most of the time you will use the component of the weight down the slope, which is $mg\sin\theta$ (where $\theta$ is the angle of incline).

### Example
(This is the same type of slope problem solved via energy in [[Energy Conservation]].)
A drunk man climbs into a supermarket trolley.
The total mass is $90\ \text{kg}$ and it is being held $40\ \text{m}$ up a slope with a $25^\circ$ incline. He is then released. There is a frictional force of $150\ \text{N}$.

1. **The weight parallel to the slope:**
   $w=mg\sin\theta$
   $w=90\times9.8\times\sin25^\circ$
   $w=373\ \text{N}$

2. **Unbalanced force:**
   $F_{\text{un}}=373-150$
   $F_{\text{un}}=223\ \text{N}$

3. **The acceleration:**
   $F_{\text{un}}=ma$
   $223=90\times a$
   $\underline{a=2.48\ \text{m/s}^2}$

4. **The speed at the bottom:**
   $v^2=u^2+2as$
   $v=\sqrt{0^2+2\times2.48\times40}$
   $\underline{v=14\ \text{m/s}}$
