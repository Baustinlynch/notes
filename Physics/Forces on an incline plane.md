 If an object is sliding down a slope we do not get the full effect of the weight, to find the force, we can [[Resolving vectors|resolve the forces]].
![[Inclined forces diagram.excalidraw]]
Most of the time you will use the component of the weight down the slope, which is $mg\sin\theta$

### Example
(This is the same type of slope problem solved via energy in [[Energy Conservation]].)
A drunk man climbs into a supermarket trolley.
The total mass is $90kg$ and it is being held $40m$ up a slope with a $25°$ incline. He is then released. There is a frictional force of $150N$.
1. **The weight parallel to the slope:**
   $w=mg\sin\theta$olumn 2
   $w=90\times9.8\times\sin25$
   $w=373N$
2. **Unbalanced force:**
   $F_{un}=373-150$
   $F_{un}=223N$
3. **The acceleration:**
   $F_{un}=ma$
   $223=90\times a$
   $a=2.48ms^{-2}$
4. **The speed at the bottom:**
   $v^2=u^2+2as$
   $v=\sqrt{0^2+2\times2.48\times40}$
   $v=14ms^{-1}$
