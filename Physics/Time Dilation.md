Moving clocks run slow. For fast-moving objects, their length contracts.

## Time Dilation
Time dilation is the phenomenon where time passes at different rates for observers in different states of motion or in different gravitational potentials.

### Special Relativistic Time Dilation
$$t = \frac{t_0}{\sqrt{1-\frac{v^2}{c^2}}}$$
Where:
- $t$ = time measured by the stationary observer (dilated time)
- $t_0$ = proper time (time measured in the moving frame)
- $v$ = relative velocity
- $c$ = speed of light

As $v$ approaches $c$, the denominator approaches 0, so $t$ becomes very large — time appears to slow down for the moving object from the stationary observer's perspective.

### Length Contraction
$$L = L_0\sqrt{1-\frac{v^2}{c^2}}$$
Where:
- $L$ = contracted length
- $L_0$ = proper length (length in the object's rest frame)
- $v$ = relative velocity
- $c$ = speed of light

Objects moving at high speeds appear shorter in the direction of motion.
