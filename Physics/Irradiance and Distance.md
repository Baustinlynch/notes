If we vary the distance of a 'point source' from a detector we produce a graph...
![[Drawing 2026-11-16 09.02.53.excalidraw]]
which we can manipulate to give...
![[Drawing 2026-11-16 09.05.52.excalidraw|327]]
$$I_{\text{rradiance}}\propto \frac{1}{d^2}$$
$$I='\text{a constant}\times \frac{1}{d^2}$$
$$I=\frac{k}{d^2}$$
$$\text{Irradiance}=\frac{\text{constant}}{d^2}$$
This **<u><b>is</b></u>** in the data sheet.
#### Example:
1. A bulb shines onto a screen of a fixed area, 3m away. $I$ at this point is $0.01Wm^{-2}$. The bulb is moved to 1.5m away, what is $I$ now?
   $Id^2=I_{2}d^2_{2}$
   $0.01\times{3}^2=I_{2}\times 1.5^2$
   $0.09=I_{2}\times 2.25$
   $I_{2}=\frac{0.01\times 3^2}{1.5^2}$
   $I_{2}=0.04Wm^{-2}$
