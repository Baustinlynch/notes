When spacecraft re-enter the atmosphere there are massive amounts of friction that create large amounts of heat. The faster something travels, the more it is impacted by friction.
(This is the [[Energy Conservation|conservation of energy]] in action — kinetic energy is converted into heat.)
$$E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 \quad \text{or} \quad E_w = Fd$$
$$E_h = cm\Delta t$$
$$E_h = mL$$
During re-entry, the spacecraft has large amounts of **kinetic energy**; it is slowed down by air resistance/friction. The kinetic energy of the spacecraft is converted into heat **through** friction, therefore slowing it down. This causes the spacecraft to get extremely hot, necessitating **very** good insulation materials in the heat shield.

> [!NOTE]
> Some of the energy may not be lost to heat but to other factors like light and sound.
