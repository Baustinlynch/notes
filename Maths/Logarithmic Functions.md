A **logarithmic function** is the **inverse** of an [[Exponential Function]]
The logarithmic function $f(x)=\log ^{}_{a}x$ is the [[Inverse Functions|Inverse Function]] to the exponential function $f(x)=a^x$ 
   
%% col-start %%

%% col-break %%

If $y=a^x$        -->

$f(x)=2^x$         -->

%% col-break:stk:1 %%

$x=\log _xy$

$f^{-1}(x)=\log _ax$

%% col-end %%

### Graphing a logarithmic function

| x                | $\frac18$ | $\frac14$ | $\frac12$ | $1$ | $2$ | $4$ | $8$ |
| ---------------- | --------- | --------- | --------- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $f(x)=\log _2 x$ | $-3$      | $-2$      | $-1$      | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ |

> [!NOTE] Important features
> Logarithmic graphs always go through $(1,0)$
> All log graphs pass through $\underline{(a,1)}$where $a$ is the base
