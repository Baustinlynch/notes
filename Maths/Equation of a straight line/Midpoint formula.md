To find the midpoint of a straight line you use the equation 
$$M(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2},\frac{y_1+y_2}{2})$$
![[Midpoint formula diagram.excalidraw]]

### For example:
Here's an example using the midpoint formula:

**Given two points:** A(2, 3) and B(8, 7)

**Apply the formula:**

$$M = \left(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right)$$

$$M = \left(\frac{2+8}{2}, \frac{3+7}{2}\right) = \left(\frac{10}{2}, \frac{10}{2}\right) = (5, 5)$$

**So the midpoint of A and B is (5, 5).**

Quick way to check it makes sense: (5, 5) should sit exactly halfway between (2, 3) and (8, 7) — and it does, since moving from A to M is +3 in x and +2 in y, and moving from M to B is another +3 in x and +2 in y. 

(Related: the [[Distance formula]] finds the distance between the same two points.)
