To find the equation of the line parallel to another line, that passes through a point, you must find the gradient of the known line, and then make an equation with the point that we know on the other line and the gradient
### Examples:
Find the equation of a line through (3,-2) parallel to the line $x+2y=7$.
**First, find the gradient of the known line;**
$x+2y=7$
$2y=7-x$
$y=\frac{7-x}{2}=\frac{7}{2}-\frac{x}{2}=\frac{7}{2}-\frac{1}{2}x$
$m=-\frac{1}{2}$

**Then find the new line;**
$y-b=m(x-a)$
**By putting the gradient and point into the equation:**
$m=-\frac{1}{2}~~~(a,b)=(3,-2)$

$y+2=-\frac{1}{2}(x-3)$
$2(y+2)=-1(x-3)$
$2y+4=-x+3$
$2y=-x-1$
