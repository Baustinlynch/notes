A quadratic equation is made up of two main parts, the **quadratic term** and the **linear term**. These parts have two main factors, **the term**, and the **coefficient** of the term. For example, in the quadratic equation $3x^2-5x+7=0$, the **quadratic term** is $3x^2$ and the **coefficient** of the quadratic term is $3$. The **linear term** is $-5x$ and the **coefficient** of the linear term is $-5$ 
When you solve a quadratic equation it will often have more than one answer. This is because you get the same answer if you square the positive or negative version of a number. e.g. $\sqrt9$ and $\sqrt-9$ both $=3$ 

# Solving simple quadratics without linear terms
A **simple quadratic** equation, with no linear term, can be solved by changing the subject of the equation. e.g.

| $x^2=9$                       | $x^2+5=11$                    | $x^2-8=0$                     |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| $x=\sqrt{9}$ or $x=-\sqrt{9}$ | $x^2=6$                       | $x^2=8$                       |
| $x=3$ or $x=-3$               | $x=\sqrt{6}$ or $x=-\sqrt{6}$ | $x=\sqrt{8}$ or $x=-\sqrt{8}$ |
You can also do the same with slightly more complicated equations

| $(x-6)^2=0$  | $(x-5)^2=81$        | $(x+7)^2=100$         |
| ------------ | ------------------- | --------------------- |
| $x-6=\sqrt0$ | $x-5=9$ or $x-5=-9$ | $x+7=10$ or $x+7=-10$ |
| $x=6$        | $x=14$ or $x=-4$    | $x=3$ or $x=-17$      |
