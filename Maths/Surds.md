A surd is a square root, cube root etc., of a whole number, which is **not** equal to a **whole number**.
All surds are **irrational numbers**, i.e. they **cannot be written as a fraction**. Therefore they also **cannot** be written as a **terminating decimal number**. (Surds also appear in [[Exact values and Radians|exact trigonometric values]].)
### Where do surds come up?
Surds come up in lots of places; some examples are:
The exact solutions of some equations e.g.
$x^2+1=6$
$x^2=5$
$\underline{x=\sqrt5}$ or $\underline{x=-\sqrt5}$

The exact length of the hypotenuse e.g.
$x^2=2^2+3^2$
$x^2=13$
$x=\sqrt{13}$
---
## Simplifying surds
To simplify a surd you find the largest square factor of the number under the root sign.
$=\sqrt{12}$
$=\sqrt{4\times3}$
$=\sqrt4\times\sqrt3$
You then turn the rational number into a whole number e.g.
$=2\times\sqrt3$
$=2\sqrt3$
---
## Adding and subtracting surds
When adding and subtracting surds the number in the square root needs to be the same. For example,
$=\sqrt{12}+\sqrt{27}$
$=2\sqrt{3}+3\sqrt{3}$
$=5\sqrt{3}$
