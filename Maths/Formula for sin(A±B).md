 $$\sin(A+B)=\sin A\cos B+\cos A\sin B$$
$$\sin(A-B)=\sin A\cos B-\cos A\sin B$$
$$\text{for any angles A,B}$$
### Sin(A-B)
This equation **is** on the formula list.
$\sin(A+(-B))=\sin A\cos(-B)+\cos A\sin(-B)$
$\sin A\cos B-\cos A\sin B$
![[Drawing 2026-11-06 09.51.08.excalidraw|470]]
#### Examples (A+B):
1. Verify the formula when $A=30\degree$ and  $B=60\degree$
   ==🔴 Check both sides **have** the same values==
   $\sin (A+B)=\sin(30+60)=\sin(90)=1$
   $\sin A\cos B+\cos A\sin B=\sin(30)\cos(60)+\cos(30)\sin(60)$
   $=\frac{1}{2}\times \frac{1}{2}+\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\times\frac{\sqrt3}{2}$
   $=\frac{1}{4}+\frac{3}{4}=1$
   ==🔴 Verified==, both sides equal 1

2. Expand $\sin(A+3B)$
   $\sin(A)\cos(3B)+\cos(A)\sin(3B)$

3. If $A$ and $B$ are acute angles with
   $\cos A=\frac{4}{5}$,  $\sin B=\frac{5}{13}$
   Find the **exact value** of $\sin(A+B)$
   ==🔴 **What do we know?**==
   $A$  and $B$ are acute, so this is a right-angled triangle.
   ![[Drawing 2026-11-09 00.09.27.excalidraw|436]]
   $\sin(A+B)=\frac{3}{5}\times \frac{12}{13}+\frac{4}{5}\times\frac{5}{13}$
   $=\frac{36}{65}+\frac{20}{65}=\frac{56}{65}$

#### Examples (A-B):

1. Simplify $\sin 130 \degree\cos 40\degree-\cos 130\degree\sin 40\degree$
   $=\sin(130-40)\degree$
   $=\sin(90)\degree$
   $=1$
2. $P$ and $Q$ are acute angles.
   $\cos P=\frac{4}{5}$, $\sin Q=\frac{5}{13}$
   **Show that** $\sin(P-Q)=\frac{16}{65}$
   $\sin(P-Q)=\sin P\cos Q-\cos P\sin Q$
   ![[Drawing 2026-11-09 00.01.59.excalidraw]]
   $=\frac{3}{5}\times \frac{12}{13}-\frac{4}{5}\times \frac{5}{13}$
   $=\frac{36}{65}-\frac{20}{65}$
   $=\frac{16}{65}$
   ==🔴 Shown==
